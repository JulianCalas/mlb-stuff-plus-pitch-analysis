# Opposing pitcher — one-page card

The card Julian wrote at Barry, filled with a major-league example so the template is not blank. One pitcher, one page, attack plan first. Numbers are 2026 regular season, John King, LHP, Miami. Stuff+ is the shape grade from the 2024–2025 model (100 = average for that pitch). Whiff is on swings. Run value by batter hand is raw Statcast `delta_run_exp` (count included), not the count-neutral model target.

**John King, LHP, MIA · 49.2 IP · ERA 3.99 · FIP 4.87 · K 16.8% · BB 8.6% · Stuff+ 92**
Low slot, about 25° arm angle, release height about 5.4 ft. The sinker and the changeup are the same pitch 7 mph apart. Do not swing at a pitch that looks like the sinker and then dives off the plate to his arm side.

## How he gets outs

He pitches backward from a soft sinker. The sinker is 90–91, +2 inches of ride, +13 inches of arm-side run, and it does not miss bats (17% whiff). The changeup is 84, almost the same break, 34% whiff, thrown in the chase zone (30% in zone). The sweeper is the glove-side breaker (−16 inches). The curve has real depth (−12 inches IVB) and a higher slot (35°), and it has been hit when put in play this year on a small sample. The four-seam is not a pitch (9 thrown).

Results have been better than the shape, and better against left-handed hitters (−1.6 runs per 100 of raw run expectancy, 405 pitches) than against right-handed hitters (−0.2, 318 pitches). Sit on the fastball. Let the changeup and the sweeper prove they are strikes.

## Attack

**Left-handed hitters (405 pitches).** Mix is sinker (131), slider (95), sweeper (77), changeup (63), curve (38). The sinker is the strike. Whiff on it is 16% (12/73 swings) and raw run value is −2.2 per 100, so it has played, but it is 90 with sinker shape from a low slot. Look away, down, and swing if it is a strike early. The slider only misses bats at 21% (8/39). The sweeper is the pitch to spit on unless it starts over the plate: −16 inches of sweep will finish off the plate. The changeup against same-side hitters still missed bats (32%, 12/37) and ran a −4.3 raw run value per 100. If it looks like the sinker and is down, take it.

**Right-handed hitters (318 pitches).** He shifts toward the changeup (80 pitches, 36% whiff, 15/42 swings, raw run value −2.1 per 100) and uses the sinker as the plate pitch (98 pitches, 17% whiff). The curve against right-handed hitters was 0 whiffs in 16 swings and +6.3 raw run value per 100 on 44 pitches. That is a small sample and it is the pitch to do damage on if it is up or early. The sweeper against right-handed hitters was good for him (−5.4 raw run value per 100) on only 37 pitches and 15 swings. Do not chase it off the plate. Make him throw the sinker over.

## Plan for the at-bat

1. First pitch, expect the sinker arm-side. It is his zone pitch (53% of sinkers are in the zone). The changeup looks identical and is in the zone only 30% of the time, across all counts.
2. Two strikes, respect the changeup down and the sweeper off the glove side. Both are the chase pitches. The slider is the one that stays in the zone without missing the bat. That is the two-strike pitch to swing through.
3. If the release is visibly higher, it is the curve. This year that has been a pitch to hit, especially right-handed, and the sample is too small to treat that as a law. It is a tendency to update, not a scouting religion.
4. Do not give him the 3.99 ERA as a reason to take a strike. FIP and the shape grade both say the ball is ordinary. Swing at the sinker in the zone.
